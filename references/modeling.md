# Modeling in Blender

`likeness tools <command>` below is `python <skill>/scripts/likeness.py <command>`.

The modules below load inside Blender as `from modeling import …`. Coordinates are in Blender units. Set the world scale from the subject before choosing radii, hair strand widths or scattering density.

## Modeling from observation

Start with what the viewer must recognize or believe and how close they can get. For a replica, separate measured features from plausible reconstructions. For an invented subject, choose real analogues for its structure, surfaces and motion. A creature can borrow a bird's balance and a mammal's fur while keeping its own silhouette. Match the agreed design even when several physical interpretations are possible.

Read the reference for how the subject carries weight, fits together, moves or reacts to light. When a draft looks wrong, choose a view or a small change that would give different results for the competing explanations. Make that change on one representative part before repeating it.

| What looks wrong | How to separate the causes | What to change |
|---|---|---|
| A glazed facade reads as a flat, dark grid. | Move sideways at eye level past one bay. Watch the slab edge, the frame and something inside. Then keep the camera fixed and vary what the pane reflects. | Absent parallax calls for real recesses, reveals or a floor and partition behind the pane. If the depth is there, balance transmission, the exterior reflection and the light reaching the interior. Use a measured bay for repetition and resolve corners and entry bays separately. |
| A product looks like molded plastic all the way around. | Rotate it against a broad light card. Inspect the outer profile, reflection width at an edge and shadow at a joint. Compare those with the same region on the real product. | Correct the profile or wall thickness when the outline is wrong. Add the bevel or gap dictated by assembly where adjoining parts merge. Tune roughness or brushing when the reflected shape is present but spreads in the wrong direction. |
| A landscape reads as a miniature or a wall of scenery. | Put the camera at the intended eye height. Move it a few steps and compare the parallax of foreground, middle ground and skyline. Look across the relief under side light. | Repair scale, grade and spacing when everything moves together. Restore separate ridges when the horizon is wrong. Adjust distant contrast only after the layers exist. Place foliage where moisture, exposure, season and use support it. |
| A character looks weightless, or loses its recognizable shape as it turns. | Inspect a flat silhouette of the loaded pose, then a front and profile at a comparable lens and expression. Bend the joint that carries the weight. | Move the pelvis or supporting foot for balance. Fit skull, jaw and neck for a persistent profile error. Correct joint placement, weights or a corrective shape when the neutral mesh only collapses under load. For another species, find references for its own gait and skeleton. |
| Cloth or another soft form looks cast in resin. | Look at the attached and free edges in the actual pose. Compare an unloaded area with a pin, seat, elbow or contact patch. Where would gravity or tension pull the folds? | Place anchors and collision surfaces first. Sculpt persistent folds for a fixed pose. Use shape keys or simulate changing contact for motion. Adjust the weave or fine wrinkles after the form responds to its load. |

Combine techniques on one object. An upholstered chair has rigid joints, a cushion that compresses under load, stitched piping and a weave that changes the highlight. A lit close-up at delivery scale tells you whether the weave needs geometry. For an unfamiliar subject, find a reference that distinguishes the competing constructions and try that feature first.

## Reference and asset sources

For downloaded assets, keep the fetch result's `source_line` with the project credits.

For a recognizable subject, start with the supplied references. For missing information, search text and images yourself using the local name, location, model number or year. Open the source page to confirm the subject and version. An architect's elevation, manufacturer's specification or dated event photo can settle questions a gallery of uncaptioned images leaves open. Pick views for distinct jobs: one for silhouette and proportions, another for depth or assembly, a close view for the identifying detail. Stop searching when those modeling decisions are settled. If a defining feature is still ambiguous, ask for the particular view that would resolve it. Infer ordinary hidden construction conservatively and distinguish it from a documented feature when fidelity matters. A generated concept can guide mood or original design; establish a replica's dimensions and markings from real references.

Choose a measured dimension or a repeated element of known size. Match the horizon, vanishing directions and camera angle before tracing proportions. For a tower, distinguish roof height from spire height. For a product, distinguish perspective taper from a change in cross-section. A different character adaptation can supply topology; fit its proportions, costume and hair to the intended version.

```text
python <skill>/scripts/sources.py accounts
python <skill>/scripts/sources.py search all "asset or landmark" --limit 4
python <skill>/scripts/sources.py search commons "architectural detail"
python <skill>/scripts/sources.py search sketchfab "asset name" --limit 8
python <skill>/scripts/sources.py search blenderkit "asset name" --kind model
python <skill>/scripts/sources.py search polyhaven "limestone" --kind textures
python <skill>/scripts/sources.py search ambientcg "stone"
python <skill>/scripts/sources.py fetch provider:id new-directory [--resolution 2k]
```

The `find_references` MCP tool searches selected catalogs together. Sketchfab and BlenderKit read `SKETCHFAB_TOKEN` and `BLENDERKIT_API_KEY`, or the corresponding `sketchfab_token` and `blenderkit_api_key` fields in the credential file selected by `BLENDER_STUDIO_ACCOUNTS`. This workspace can point at an existing credential file through its private runtime's `asset-accounts.path`. Results include the author, license and source URL. Inspect the actual downloaded geometry, textures, scale and rig before choosing it.

## People and creatures

Choose topology for the next operation. Project Heist's sculpting base uses fairly even quads, few poles and real-world scale so Multires subdivisions and smoothing behave predictably. For a production rig, preserve useful loops around the mouth, eyes and joints, test the required pose and keep high-frequency sculpting on a compatible layer or a separate mesh. The downloadable Heist example and the CC0 bundle below have their own licenses. [Realistic Character Workflow](https://studio.blender.org/training/realistic-human-research/) · [Use of Base Meshes](https://studio.blender.org/training/realistic-human-research/use-of-base-meshes/)

- For a posed realistic adult male, `assets.append_base("Realistic Male - Rigged")` (13 MB, downloaded on first use) loads the complete neutral body at the origin with a 51-bone deform skeleton, finger weights, parented eyes and multiresolution sculpt levels. Pose the armature, fit the body on a shape key and add the subject’s complexion and wardrobe. The starter uses a neutral clay material.
- For a fresh sculpt or a different skeleton, append the CC0 Blender Studio Human Base Meshes v1.4.1 collection (downloaded into the library on first use, 51 MB) with `assets.append_base("Body Male - Realistic")` or `assets.append_base("Body Female - Realistic")`. The realistic male asset contains one continuous body with ears, throat, hands and feet, two parented eyes, UVs and a multiresolution sculpt. Its stock body has no deform armature or skin texture. Move its root once to clear the catalog offset. Fit a compatible skeleton or bind the body before posing it. The separate anatomical skeleton collection is a visual bone model. [Official distribution](https://download.blender.org/demo/asset-bundles/human-base-meshes/)
- For a photographed person in existing clothes, a scanned figure can supply the asymmetry of the face, skin and drape. Choose a rigged scan when the pose must change, inspect whether the clothing or light is baked into its texture, and check the asset terms before bundling the editable mesh. [Renderpeople scan samples](https://renderpeople.com/free-3d-people/)
- Shape the body copy to the subject in its neutral pose, retaining the face loops and ear-to-jaw-to-neck transition. Keep proportion or expression changes editable. Use the front photo for width and spacing and an oblique or side view for projection, ear attachment and haircut. Weight each photo toward the surfaces its camera actually sees. Check the untextured profile and back of the neck, then the dressed figure at hands, sleeve cuffs, knees and foot contact in the intended pose. A clothing mask can conceal covered anatomy while preserving the complete body for another outfit.
- Establish silhouette and face depth in front, profile and three-quarter views. Treat eyes, lids, lips, inner mouth, ears and hairline as separate anatomical forms. Fit the jaw, cheek and brow before adding skin grain. Use `sculpt.Sculptor(obj)`, its local-space `grab`, `crease_line`, masks and `commit()` for controlled edits. Preserve existing shape keys and the deforming mesh; keep topology-changing work on a separate sculpt.
- For a new deformation mesh use `retopo.setup_cage`, `add_ring`, `bridge`, `project` and `relax`. Direct loops around the mouth and lids, and across moving joints. For a humanoid needing a new rig, `rig.landmarks`, `add_metarig`, `fit_metarig`, `generate` and `bind` work on an actual body mesh. Inspect fit, weights and the required pose. Keep existing character-specific rigs and fit a creature's skeleton to its own joints.
- If an unanimated exchange rig looks right at rest but explodes on the first joint bend, `pose.rebind_visible_pose(rig)` can make that visible neutral shape its bind pose in a separate working file. It preserves vertex weights and UVs and rejects rigs with animation, constraints or shape keys. Use `pose.reach(rig, upper, lower, end, target, pole)` for a hand on a knee or a foot on a step. Target and pole are world-space points; choose the pole on the intended side of the elbow or knee.
- Make a haircut readable as masses from the back and profile. Use `hair.new_groom`, `sample_roots`, `add_guides` and `build_stack` on a UV-mapped scalp for strands, or `hair.from_paths(name, paths, radii, material, parent)` for strands computed as arrays. Use modeled locks for graphic styles. Establish the silhouette, parting, hairline and gravity before adding flyaways. Try the requested action on the final rig; for dialogue include a lid closure and the needed mouth shapes.
- `shading.skin(color_image=…, normal_image=…, sss_scale=0.012)`, `shading.eye(iris_color, iris_image)`, `shading.teeth()` and `shading.gums()` are the character materials; `shading.simple(name, color, roughness, metallic)` is a plain one.
- For a realistic portrait, compare the untextured face under a broad side light at the matched lens. Check how the lids wrap the eyeball, where the cheek turns into the jaw and how the lips project in profile. In the finished material, vary roughness across forehead, cheeks and lips and judge the corneal highlight and tear line at portrait distance. For a full figure, read the weight on the supporting foot and the pull of cloth from shoulders, elbows and hips before spending time on pores or stitch patterns.
- `mesh.world_geometry(obj)` gives evaluated positions and normals as arrays, `mesh.surface(obj, origin, axes)` a BVH with per-corner UVs in the subject's own frame, `mesh.write_field` and `read_field` named attributes, `mesh.rbf_field` smooth interpolation between anchors. On a bound mesh, `rigged.shape_from_world_delta(obj, name, delta)` turns displacements measured in the posed world into a rest-space shape key; `rigged.find_bone(rig, "DEF-spine.006")` accepts a prefix or pattern and `rigged.attach(obj, rig, bone)` parents without moving.

## Likeness from photographs

Measure instead of guessing. Each step is a build script the commands write for you, so it stays in the project's record.

1. `sources.py landmarks photo.jpg` reports the named face landmarks a photograph contains. Choose a front portrait and a profile or three-quarter view with a neutral expression.
2. `likeness tools match-photo model.blend photo.jpg next.blend --head "Head" --name "Photo | front"` finds the same landmarks on a render of the head, remembers which vertices they are, solves the camera that took the photograph and adds it to the scene with the photo as its background. The result reports the fit in pixels. Repeat per photograph; later photographs reuse the stored vertices.
3. `likeness tools compare next.blend photo.jpg out.png --angle camera --camera "Photo | front" --overlay` renders through that camera and adds a blended panel; the landmark error is included.
4. `likeness tools fit-face next.blend fitted.blend --head "Head" --camera "Photo | front" --camera "Photo | profile" --transfer "Brows" --move "Eyes"` moves the landmarks to where every photo sees them and interpolates smoothly between them as a shape key. Hair, brows, lashes and stubble listed with `--transfer` deform with the skin; eyeballs, corneas and teeth listed with `--move` shift as pieces. The result reports the error before and after per camera. One front and one side photograph settle width, height and depth; a third view checks.
5. Look at the overlays, then refine locally with `sculpt.Sculptor` where the photographs disagree with the mesh beyond the landmarks: jaw corner, cheek fullness, nose bridge.

`fit.landmark_error`, `fit.fit_to_photos`, `fit.transfer_delta` and `fit.photo_camera` are the functions behind these commands.

When refining a previously fitted likeness, measure the current render first. Old landmark deltas describe the mesh revision they were measured on and may already be baked into an active shape key. Before picking a feature, compare the whole head with independently photographed references at comparable scale and pitch. Use a second angle to establish depth and several near-frontal expressions to see which shapes persist. For a neutral mouth, compare closed-mouth widths and the height of the corners relative to the lip seam, scaled by pupil spacing. Keep a smile as an expression reference. Closely cropped thumbnails help find candidates; prefer a traceable original for detail. A close fit to one capture establishes that expression only. On a posed rig, sculpt in head-relative coordinates and transfer the displacement to an additional shape key. Render both states from the same cameras with identical light, crop and image scale so the local change is visible.

## Machines, props and environments

- `profiles` provides cross-sections. `parts.lathe`, `extrude`, `shell`, `sweep`, `opening`, `knurl`, `fillet` and `place` build actual shells, holes, fittings and contact points. Keep moving parts separate with origins at their axes. Use `shading` for brushed metal, glass, cloth, leather and surface wear. `lighting.studio_rig` provides a product light setup when needed.
- For a building replica, match the ground plan, relative heights, setbacks and gaps between volumes to the sightline. In a skyline a roof profile or the opening under a bridge can identify the building. At pedestrian distance, take a section through a bay and follow the construction from exterior face to occupied space. Check it at the nearest approach and from a corner before duplicating it. For a product, follow the assembly from shell to insert, lid or handle. Set component origins and clearances from the real mechanism.
- For environments, establish human scale, the viewing route and the nearest detailed surface. Give silhouette-changing detail real geometry. Use `layout.seat(obj, surface, local_anchor)` to seat a defined root on evaluated terrain. `layout.scatter(surface, prototypes, density, …)` places collection variants through editable Geometry Nodes and accepts a painted density field. Author rooted variants around their origins; use separate plants in front of a close camera where the exact shape matters.
- For a new material graphic use `assets.apply_color_art(obj, slot_index, image_path, uv_name, mask_path=None)`. A small graphic can be placed at a clicked surface with `center_uv=hit["uv"][uv_name], size_uv=(width, height), angle=radians`. The named material slot is local to that object. Choose the affected UV islands deliberately. For export to a simpler material format, bake the combined surface to the export UVs.

## Asset exchange and baking

- Use `assets.pbr_material(folder, name=None, scale=1, displacement=False)` on a downloaded PBR texture set. It recognizes color, roughness, metallic, AO, opacity, OpenGL and DirectX normals, and height. Assign the result to a UV-mapped mesh. Use displacement when the destination surface has enough geometry.
- `assets.import_model(path, target_size=None, name=None, collection=None, credit=None)` imports glTF/GLB, FBX, OBJ, USD or STL into a named collection. Its origin sits at the bottom center and target size is the largest dimension in meters. The result includes objects, origin, collection, face and material counts, and dimensions in meters. Pass name, author, license and URL in `credit`. Use `assets.append_from(path, names=None, collection=None, link=False)` for objects or collections from another Blender file. Set `link=True` to retain the external library.
- `assets.unwrap(obj, method="smart", margin=.02, seams_from_sharp=True, uv_name="UVMap")` creates or updates a named UV map. Use `angle` for a mesh with seams. Before a selected-to-active bake, align the high and low surfaces in world space and give the low mesh UVs. `assets.bake(high, low, maps=("normal","ao","diffuse"), size=2048, cage_extrusion=.02, folder=...)` saves new PNGs, packs them and leaves labeled image nodes in the low mesh's material. Connect tangent-space normals through a Normal Map node.
- `likeness tools export model.blend output.glb|.fbx|.usdc|.stl` accepts an object or collection with `--subject`. GLB embeds supported textures and applies mesh modifiers. FBX accepts `--apply-modifiers`. Keep the named textures folder beside a USDC file. For STL, use closed surfaces and `--scale 1000` to convert meters to millimeters. For exact painted or procedural shading in another renderer, bake the needed maps.

## Environments

In *Color Void*, the track width came from satellite imagery, onboard footage informed the tilt and slope, and a camera on the blocked car exposed what needed adjusting. Trees and bushes used Geometry Nodes, while set dressing was judged from the camera. For a new route, take a scale cue and an eye-level reference, try the actual motion early and place individual foreground elements where their parallax matters. For free exploration, repeat from the reverse approach and nearest accessible side. [Color Void production breakdown](https://studio.blender.org/blog/color-void-production-process/)

- Shape terrain from the process that formed it. For a specific mountain range, use elevation data when available and check the ridgeline from the chosen bearing. For dunes, orient the ridges to a common wind, with long windward slopes and shorter lee faces. Use noise for smaller variations after the landform reads. At a shoreline, expose rock or sediment at the waterline and follow the grade with the path and its supports.
- Distribute plants by moisture, slope, exposure and disturbance. Leave worn paths clear, cluster undergrowth where it can grow, and vary age, spacing and canopy shapes within each species. Place the few trees that frame the subject by hand, then instance the background. Scanned vegetation, rocks and ground textures help at close range when their species, scale and season fit the setting.
- Carry weather through the scene. Snow collects on upward-facing ledges and branches, exposed steep rock can remain visible, wet paving reflects the bright sky in shallow depressions, and sheltered patches can stay dry. For a journey between climates, blend ground cover, vegetation, visibility and skylight along the approach. Check the view entering and leaving a stop as well as the best frame; a nearby tree can hide the landmark for most of the walk.

- `outdoor.ground`, `plant`, `strew`, `sky`, `haze` and `film_settings` are the lakeside film's ground, planting, light and air as functions; `outdoor.library(id)` gives the path of an asset already on this machine. `examples/starter/build.py` uses them all.
- `terrain.ground(size, resolution, height, seed, octaves, roughness, flatten)` creates editable noise relief. A clearing is `((x, y), radius)` in ground-local coordinates. Use `layout.seat` with a root's contact point to place it. `terrain.rock(size, seed, detail)` starts at its bottom center. `terrain.water((width, depth), level, wave_scale, color)` adds a water surface. `terrain.sky()` sets daylight with angles in degrees. Use `kind="hdri", hdri=path` for an environment image.
- `plants.tree(height, trunk_radius, levels, branches, seed, leaf_card, leaf_density)` returns a trunk with an instanced canopy. A supplied leaf card uses its alpha and UVs. `plants.bush(size, seed)` adds a low branching shrub. `plants.grass(surface, density, height, mask)` scatters tufts using an optional named density field.
- `building.wall(path, height, thickness, openings)` follows an XY path. Repeat the first point to close it. Describe each opening as (center distance along the path, bottom, width, height), in meters. `window` and `door` face local +Z, with +Y up and their origin at the bottom center. Use `parts.place(part, point, outward_normal)` on a wall. `stairs`, `slab`, `column` and `railing` use their base as the vertical reference. Roofs accept a simple footprint, pitch in degrees, overhang and thickness. Choose `gable`, `hip` or `flat`.

## Shape, materials and light

- Place the camera at the intended viewing height and distance. At thumbnail size, look for the shape the eye goes to first, clear space around its outline, and separation between foreground, subject and background. For an orbit or freely explorable model, turn around it and bring the nearest accessible side up to the same standard. For a measured replica, resolve overlaps by moving the camera or incidental dressing while preserving the subject's dimensions.
- Use geometry where a detail changes the outline, parallax, contact or cast shadow. Use normals and roughness for shallow relief. Set texture scale from something measurable, such as the width of a plank or a paving joint. Place wear where the surface is handled, scuffed, sheltered or exposed to runoff. In a close view, the foot of a post meeting gravel or the lip of a metal shell catching a narrow highlight often contributes more than another layer of noise.
- Read the reference light from shadow direction, edge softness and reflections. For a landscape, place the sun across the ridges when the reference shows alternating lit slopes and gullies. Balance skylight in the shadows and let distance soften the farther layers. Under overcast skies use a broad sky source, soft contact shadows and reflections consistent with that sky. For a face or product, move a large area light until its reflection describes the cheek or curved housing. Adjust fill to preserve the shadow-side form. Set exposure while retaining texture in the brightest important surface.
- Light the first blocked shot once the main forms and basic shaders exist. Compare a representative frame with the adjoining shots for the direction of the key, intensity, color and screen readability. Scrub through foreground crossings and transitions at playback speed. A lamp that describes the form in one still may hide an important feature halfway through a move. [Blender Studio shot lighting](https://studio.blender.org/tools/pipeline-overview/shot-production/lighting)
- A Workbench `clay` view shows the form under studio shading. To study actual cast shadows, use a disposable scene or view layer with a matte material override and render through the named camera with the scene lights. Preserve transmitting objects when glass or foliage controls which surfaces the light reaches. Use the original materials for reflection, transmission and roughness. Keep camera, frame, lighting, color management and crop fixed for a revision comparison. If a preview reports temporary lighting, establish the intended lighting before judging it.
- For a local sculpt or paint edit, call `show_model(source="live", camera="Review", frame=…)` on the open working copy. Pick a point inside the feature, with x and y measured as fractions from the upper left. Call `locate_surface(capture_id=…, point=[x,y])`; a selection attached by the viewer can also pass its object key. Check the returned object, world position and normal. Face that surface in the 3D viewport before a projected stroke so another fold or the far side cannot intercept it. The `modeling.gui` helpers provide `set_view`, `activate_brush` and `stroke` with a real VIEW_3D context. Use `likeness tools brush` on the named file, inspect the next live image from the same camera, then keep the edit or undo it in Blender. Use background Python for edits that only need the data API.
